These tools let you explore the math behind AI systems in your browser — the word embeddings, neural networks, risk scores, and fairness metrics that show up in court decisions, regulatory proposals, and policy debates. No installation required; everything runs locally in your browser.

I designed the tools to be used as companions to law school courses on AI, but the subject is not lawyer-specific. The word embeddings here are the same technology inside every large language model. The neural network trainer demonstrates a process underpinning most modern machine learning systems. The fairness tradeoffs apply to any classifier making consequential decisions. Anyone thinking seriously about AI--whether from law, policy, computer science, or elsewhere--should find something useful.

Although I use these in conjunction with my co-authored casebook, this is an independent project, and I have released all the source code under an open source MIT license, free for all to use, adapt, or extend. I'd welcome feedback, bug reports, and suggestions for new tools via email at ohm@law.georgetown.edu. You can send me something on social media, but I won't see it.

The tools were built in summer 2026 using Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding assistant. I designed the pedagogy and made all the substantive decisions; Claude Code did most of the typing.
